# Conquest

Conquest is a Battlefield-style mode for two 768 × 80 × 768 battlefields: **Frontier** (the default, a river valley, see [docs/maps/frontier.md](maps/frontier.md)) and **Redrock Canyon** (a red-rock desert held by a dam, see [docs/maps/redrock.md](maps/redrock.md)). Up to 16 players and bots form two teams. The internal team ids are `alpha` and `bravo`, but the game shows them as **WEST** (own team blue `#4cc3ff`) and **EAST** (enemy orange `#ff8a3d`), so flag "A" is never confused with a team. The server decides everything: capture, tickets, spawns, kits, vehicle damage and score. The client only presents snapshot fields and server events.

Every tuning value is data:

- mode rules: `CONQUEST_RULES` in `shared/conquest-contract.js`;
- kits: `KITS` (contract) and `shared/conquest-kits.js`;
- hulls, seats, mounts and anchors: `shared/vehicle-defs.js`;
- weather states, multipliers and the forecast timeline: `WEATHER_STATES` and `WEATHER_RULES` in `shared/conquest-weather.js`;
- damage multipliers: `shared/vehicle-armor.js`; the tank's hit parts, slope, ricochets and subsystems: `shared/tank-armor.js`.

Change those tables, not the logic.

## Rules

| | |
|---|---|
| Tickets | 300 per team. A death costs 1 ticket; a revive refunds it. |
| Bleed | Only a team holding at least 3 of the 5 flags drains the other: 1 ticket every 3 s (3 flags), 2 s (4) or 1 s (5). |
| End | A team at 0 tickets loses. After 20 minutes the higher ticket count wins. On equal tickets the team with more flags wins; still equal is a draw. |
| Flags | A and B start WEST, D and E start EAST, C (Iron Bridge) starts neutral. The HQs can't be captured. |
| Capture | Each flag has a control value from −1 to 1. Inside the radius, and within 8 m vertically, the side with more people moves control at `min(1 + 0.5·(net − 1), 2.5)` × the base rate (8 s per half). So 2 v 0 is 1.5×, 3 v 0 is 2×, and 4 v 0 or more is the 2.5× cap; 3 v 1 counts as net 2, so 1.5×. Equal non-zero counts contest the flag and freeze control. An empty flag drifts back toward its owner. Crossing 0 neutralizes the flag; reaching ±1 captures it. Taking an enemy flag alone takes 16 s. |
| Who counts | Living infantry, crew of ground vehicles, and crew of landed aircraft. Airborne aircraft crew and downed players don't count. |
| Restricted zones | Leaving the combat area, or an enemy entering an HQ, starts a 10 s countdown, then death (kill key `restricted`). Aircraft crew ignore both; on Frontier they fly over an airspace apron beyond the map and run the same 10 s countdown outside the combat airspace (480 m past every edge), which destroys the aircraft with its crew (see Airspace). |

The flag states are `idle`, `capturing`, `neutralizing`, `contested` and `restoring`. The server emits `flag_state` on every state change, plus exactly one `flag_neutralized` or `flag_captured` per transition. The legacy `flag_capture` event no longer exists. `ticket_low` fires once each at 25 % and at 10 %.

## Deploy, squads and kits

**Deploy before the first spawn.** A human never spawns without a deploy choice. The lobby gates every admission (`ConquestPolicy.gateDeploy`: lobby join, match start, a mid-match join and its bot takeover): instead of the immediate HQ spawn of a join, a lobby team change or a rematch, the human waits unspawned on the deploy screen. The server holds the body dead at the team's HQ with no death, ticket, kill-feed row or event, and a live body inherited from a bot leaves its seat and the field. DEPLOY spawns it at once at the chosen point through the same deploy intent and respawn path as after a death. The deploy timeout (15 s, then the last valid choice or HQ) also applies, but its clock only starts with the client's first input, so a slow map load never auto-deploys a player who has not seen the screen yet. The client takes such a dead row without a death event and without a new death as "not spawned yet" (`LocalPlayer.holdUnspawned`): no head camera, gore, death sound or killer card. Bots and engine-level fixtures (`GameEngine.addClient` without the lobby) still spawn at HQ at once. Covered by `tools/conquest-deploy-test.mjs` (engine) and `tools/conquest-lobby-test.mjs` (real server, start and mid-match join); captures in `docs/design/conquest/deploy-first/`.

The round starts with every bot at its HQ. After each death, the **deploy screen** replaces the spectator overlay. It shows the map with every valid spawn, a kit picker (the picked class's variant toggle and, for the Engineer and the Recon, gadget toggle sit in one loadout slot under the class row, so picking a class never moves the rows or the Deploy button), a killer card and a countdown to the respawn time (6 s). If no choice arrives within 15 s of that time, the server deploys the player at the last valid choice, or else at HQ.

**Killcam.** A human killed by another player (bots included) first sees the killcam replay, then the deploy screen (it stays closed while the replay and its head-flight intro run, and opens the moment the replay ends or is skipped with Space, pad A or the SKIP button). The respawn time is unchanged: the replay window (`KILLCAM.respawnMs`, 4.8 s from the death) ends inside the 6 s delay, so the deploy countdown still reads the authoritative `respawnAt`. A killer seated in a hull is replayed from that seat's chase view (gunners over their gun, the chin gunner through its sight) with the recorded hulls drawn; the kill event names the hull and seat (`kv`, `ks`). A redeploy (kill key `redeploy`) and deaths without a killer show no replay. A medic revive during the replay ends it.

Spawn options come from `deployOptions` in `shared/conquest.js`. The server validates choices with the same function the client uses to list them.

- **HQ**: always available, with 2 s of spawn protection.
- **Flag**: a flag your team owns that isn't being neutralized or contested and has no enemy inside it. The server picks one of the flag's 12 spawn cells. It skips cells an enemy within 40 m can see and prefers cells away from enemy-held flags.
- **Squad mate**: a squad mate who is alive, not damaged in the last 4 s, and not on a flag being taken from your team. You spawn 1.5–3.5 m behind them, or just outside the hull when they ride a ground vehicle; if no cell there is free, the choice is refused as `busy`. Each mate can be spawned on once per 10 s. A mate in an aircraft seats you in that aircraft instead (a free seat, else a seat a bot holds; "IN ATTACK HELI · GUNNER"); with no such seat the mate is `busy`.
- **Spawn beacon**: a squad Recon's SPAWN BEACON (`beacon:<id>`, the Recon itself included). You spawn 1.2–3 m from it with no spawn protection; with no free cell there it is `busy`. It is refused as `enemy` while a living enemy stands within 20 m of it and as `cooldown` for 10 s after each spawn on it (the row reads "VALE · READY IN 4 S"). See *Recon gadgets*.
- **Vehicle seat**: a free seat in a friendly hull that is neither destroyed nor disabled. A human may also pick a seat a friendly **bot** holds: the deploy screen lists it as "TAKE SEAT · BOT-7" (a dashed TAKE chip per bot seat), and deploying there puts the bot out (see *Seat takeover*). Without a named seat a free seat comes first, then the first bot seat in F-key order. Bots never deploy into a bot seat, and nobody ever takes a human's seat.

**Redeploy (RESPAWN).** While alive in a live Conquest match, the in-game menu (Esc, the touch pause button) shows a **RESPAWN** button between RESUME and QUIT ("Counts as a death · opens deploy"). It sends the `redeploy` intent and closes the menu without re-locking the pointer. The server (`ConquestPolicy.redeploy`) kills the player where they are with the kill key `redeploy`: an ordinary death that costs 1 ticket, counts on the scoreboard and uses the normal respawn delay. The body is not revivable. If an enemy damaged the player within the last 5 s (`FALL_DAMAGE.creditMs`, the fall-death rule), that enemy gets the kill, so a redeploy never denies a kill; otherwise there is no killer. The kill feed shows the redeploy icon (two circling arrows) and the killer card reads "YOU DIED · REDEPLOYED" (or names the credited enemy). A seated player dies in the seat: the seat is freed like any crew death and the hull carries on (an aircraft without its pilot flies on as after an ejection). The deploy screen opens on the death and frees the mouse. The server refuses a redeploy while dead or down, outside the live phase, and within 10 s of the last one (`CONQUEST_RULES.redeployCooldownMs`). A refused send brings the menu back after 1.5 s. Only Conquest offers the button.

An invalid choice returns `deploy_refused {reason}` and keeps the screen open (a beacon reads BEACON COOLING DOWN, ENEMIES NEAR THE BEACON or NO ROOM AT THE BEACON). The possible reasons are `invalid`, `contested`, `enemy`, `busy`, `cooldown`, `seat` and `locked` (a class above the player's career level; the event adds `kit` and `level`, shown as "CLASS LOCKED · LV 5"; a vehicle upgrade above it adds `vehicle`, `upgrade` and `level` instead, shown as "TANK THERMAL OPTICS LOCKED · LV 8"). A flag whose spawn cells are all buried or cratered is refused as `invalid`; `enemy` means usable cells exist but enemies can see them.

Squads of up to 4 are filled automatically in join order. The longest-standing member leads.

Nine kits (`KITS` in `shared/conquest-contract.js`, tuning in `KIT_ROLE_RULES` in `shared/conquest-kits.js`). Five base kits are always open; four more open with the career level (the curve is `level = 1 + floor(sqrt(xp / 100))`, so LV 3 = 400 XP, LV 5 = 1,600, LV 7 = 3,600, LV 9 = 6,400).

**Unlocks.** The server holds the level: admission reads it from the profile (`meta.careerLevel`), the lobby stamps `entity.careerLevel` at admission and at match start, and every level-up during play is pushed to the live entity (levels never drop). Guests without a career are level 1; bots ignore the gate. The server announces the open kits with `kit_unlocks {id, level, unlocked, newly}` (on join, at match start, on a rematch and on a level-up that opens a kit); the client keeps the latest own event, buffered from the first snapshots while the map still loads, and the server restates it on every human death (when the deploy screen opens). Only a later rise of an already stamped level fills `newly`: joining at level 9 announces the open kits without a banner. A deploy intent with a locked kit is refused (`locked`); a remembered locked kit (a taken-over bot's) falls back to the Assault, at a timed deploy and at every respawn the policy issues itself (rematch, lobby team change). A level gained mid-match applies at the next deploy, and `newly` shows the banner "NEW CLASS UNLOCKED · PYRO" (while the deploy screen is open, its UNLOCKS header and the header status say "NEW CLASS · PYRO" instead, the status until the class is picked or the screen closes, and a tap on that status scrolls to the UNLOCKS row; HUD banners stay hidden under the screen). The career page lists the four unlocks as display-only level rewards. Conquest teamplay earns career XP (`CAREER_REWARDS.conquestObjective`): 15 XP per revive, capture, neutralize and defend, 3 XP per heal, repair and resupply award (at most 30 XP per minute), on top of the kill, minute and match XP. Ribbons pay 20 XP and medals 50 XP each (`conquestRibbon`, `conquestMedal`, see *Ribbons and medals*).

**Deploy picker.** The deploy screen shows the kits as a two-row strip under the map and the spawn list (`KIT_MENU_ORDER`): CLASSES (Assault, Medic, Engineer, Support, Recon) and UNLOCKS (Pyro, Grenadier, Raider, Marksman); phones use a two-column grid inside the panel's own scroller. A locked card is dimmed and dashed with a padlock, "LV n" and a thin XP bar ("1,240 / 1,600 XP" from the career view); clicking it says "GRENADIER UNLOCKS AT LV 5 · 1,240 / 1,600 XP" in the class info line under the base row for 4 s and selects nothing, and gamepad LB / RB skip it. A stored locked preference falls back to the Assault. The class info line shows the ability hint of the hovered, focused or selected class, formatted from `KIT_ROLE_RULES` (`kitAbilityHint`). A class a level-up just opened carries a NEW corner badge until picked. Layout: on desktop the map and spawn list shrink with the window height, and DEPLOY sits beside the UNLOCKS row, so everything fits a 1280×720 window; below 1024 px wide DEPLOY drops to its own row under the UNLOCKS row (which then takes the full width) and the loadout strip drops its class name; in phone landscape the classes are a third column that scrolls on its own beside the map and spawns; in phone portrait the map is smaller, the spawn list scrolls, the picked class spans both columns and the header (under the pause button) stays put while the panel scrolls. The status line (refusals, revive wait, auto-deploy, a class unlocked while the screen is open) sits in the header; on phones in portrait it keeps a fixed two-line row even while empty, so a status appearing mid-choice never moves the classes. Design references and captures: `docs/design/conquest/classes/`.

**Medic HUD.** Only a Medic gets the revive hold; anyone else over a downed mate sees "MERCER NEEDS A MEDIC" (no key). A Medic sees downed mates up to 60 m (others 40 m), a green cross over mates under 60 % HP within 40 m and a dashed heal-aura ring around itself on the minimap. A healed player sees a short green "+5 HP" beside the health row (inside the HP card on touch), driven by the server `heal {id, by, hp}` event. While down, the deploy status names the nearest friendly Medic and its distance ("AWAITING REVIVE · NEAREST MEDIC BRANNOCK · 23 M"). An Assault whose medkit comes back sees "ADRENALINE · MEDKIT RE-ARMED".

**Bots** ignore the unlocks and fill each squad from the shared slot table `BOT_SQUAD_SLOTS`: Assault, Medic, Engineer and one flex kit picked per team, squad and match from `BOT_FLEX_POOL` (Support twice, Recon, Pyro, Grenadier, Raider, Marksman). Variants split by a stable id hash (Pyro bots always carry the flamethrower). Kit slots count the squad's bots only, so a human in the squad does not cost it its Medic, and bots that just spawned take their slot kit as soon as the roster is complete (their opening life is not the default Assault). Medic bots look for bodies within 40 m whose down window still outlasts a 6 m/s run plus the 1.2 s hold (`reviveRunInTime`), walk the last metres shooting on the move even mid-fight (they never stand still short of the body), stop once well inside the 2 m reach, and finish a revive before a low-HP fallback, walking the last 8 m to the body first (`MEDIC_BOT_RULES.reviveCommit`; the run-time check only gates a new run, so a revive under way is finished while the window lasts); with no body, a Medic walks to the most hurt teammate under 90 % HP within 45 m (12 m while it stands in a flag being taken or fought over) and stands on its heels inside the heal aura. Otherwise it trails a mate (*Medic bots* below). Pyro bots duel at the stream's reach whatever is drawn (they close to about 19 m), sprint at an infantry contact within 55 m with the flamethrower out, burn inside 31 m of the 32 m reach in 0.6-1.2 s sweeps (`FLAME_FIRE_RANGE`), draw it again from the revolver inside 30 m and take the revolver only past 38 m when not closing in; Assault bots use the medkit below 50 HP after 2 s without contact.

Every kit also carries the revolver and the melee weapon.

**Engineer gadgets.** With the Engineer picked, the loadout slot has a second toggle beside the primaries: **AT** (RX-8 HAVOC) or **AA** (AX-9 STINGER). The choice rides the deploy intent as `gadget` (0 or 1, default 0, so older clients keep the AT launcher) and the server issues exactly that launcher: an AA Engineer owns no AT rockets and the other way round (`KITS.engineer.gadgets`, `kitLoadout(kit, variant, gadget)` in `shared/conquest-kits.js`). Kits without a choice refuse `gadget: 1`. The Support aura also hands back one gadget round per mate at most every 12 s (`KIT_ROLE_RULES.gadgetResupplyMs`), up to the issued total. Key 3 raises whichever gadget the kit carries (see *Controls*).

- **RX-8 HAVOC (AT)**: dumb-fire, 42 m/s with a little drop. Aiming down sights enters its 2.5× launcher sight: a rangefinder readout (distance to the terrain, hull or soldier under the aim point; water is ranged through) and holdover bars for 50 / 100 / 150 m (a centre hold falls about 1.8 / 6.9 / 15.4 m; the rocket self-destructs at about 167 m), plus an impact diamond where the rocket would land (docs/development.md, *RX-8 launcher sight*). A direct hit deals 320 + 60 splash at the `at` class (`CONQUEST_ROCKET_PROFILE`), so a full tank takes **4 hits from the front, 3 on the side and 2 in the rear** square to the plate (the first rear hit also disables it); steep plates cost more hits or bounce the rocket off, see *Tank armour*. A jeep dies to one hit, a helicopter to three (air ×0.8). Infantry splash is ×0.7.
- **AT MINE** (both gadget choices): three anti-vehicle mines in the grenade pouch (type `atmine`, appended to `GRENADE_TYPE_IDS`, `kitOnly`, so no other kit, mode, Chaos shop or Bastion armory issues it). See *AT mines* below.
- **AX-9 STINGER (AA)**: kit-only (`WEAPONS.stinger.gadgetOnly`, never in a free roster or weapon wheel). Aim down the sights at an airborne enemy aircraft to build the lock (320 m, 7° cone, 1.4 s). The trigger only releases on a complete lock; client and server both refuse an unlocked launch. The missile (120 m/s, no gravity, proportional navigation, 3 m proximity fuse, `STINGER_RULES` in `shared/vehicle-defs.js`) deals per-airframe damage at the `aa` class: helicopter 360, transport 330, jet 170, so **2 hits kill a helicopter or a transport and 3 a jet**. Flares decoy it. It never damages ground hulls. A killed player stays **down** for 8 s and can be revived until they deploy. Nobody can be revived after dying in an exploding vehicle, from `restricted`, or by falling into the void.

**AT mines.** Rules in `shared/at-mine-rules.js` (`AT_MINE_RULES`, placement and hitbox), the hull trigger in `shared/at-mine-trigger.js`, the infantry blast in `GRENADE_TYPES.atmine`, the authority in `ProjectileSystem._placeAtMine` / `_stepAtMine` (`server/sim/projectiles.js`). The mine lives in the grenade pouch rather than a fourth weapon slot, so it reuses the claymore's placement plumbing: ready it in the pouch (or the unbound *Quick grenade: AT mine* key) and press G to lay it. G places the mine and never throws it. It goes on the floor face under the crosshair within 3 m, or, when you look ahead, onto the floor 1.2 m in front (the eye must see that spot). With no floor it reads NO GROUND and spends nothing. A ghost disc shows where it will land. The mine **arms after 1.5 s**, with an amber blinking lamp while arming and a click when it goes live. After that only a **live enemy ground hull** sets it off: wheeled or tracked handling (the jeep, the tank and any hull variant on those chassis), whose collider footprint covers the mine at ground height (along the hull it fires only once the mine is `triggerInset` 0.6 m inside the front or rear edge, so the charge goes off under the belly rather than under the nose lip; across the hull the 0.15 m `footprintMargin` still widens it). Infantry walk over it safely, as in Battlefield, and so do aircraft (even landed), wrecks and **friendly hulls**. Friendly armour can drive through its own minefield. The armour model already refuses friendly damage, so the trigger keys on the owner's team, recorded at placement. The belly charge deals **400 at the `at` class from below**. On a tank that is the `hull-bottom` plate (AT ×1.5, square on): about 600 of 1000 HP plus about 18 of splash, and it disables the hull, so **one mine cripples a tank and two destroy it**. A mine under a track does the same; one at the very edge of the skirt only breaks the tracks (about 400). Two mines laid on one spot or side by side kill a tank in one pass; a pair laid in line along the road wastes the second, which the first blast chain-fires (4.4 m reach) before the hull reaches it (about 630 in all). Measured numbers in *Vehicle balance*. A **jeep dies to one**. The infantry blast is 150 over 5.5 m (falloff ^1.22, roughly 117 at 1 m and 19 at 4.5 m). It carves a 3.2 m crater and deals the usual `explosive` splash to other hulls. Kills credit the owner with the `atmine` key (kill feed: AT MINE). An AT mine is also an explosive: a bullet hit on the 0.48 × 0.14 m disc or a nearby blast (chain reach) sets it off, and it disappears if its floor voxel is destroyed. **Limits**: at most **3 live mines per player** (a fourth removes the oldest). Mines survive the owner's death and respawns and vanish when the owner leaves the match or the round ends. The mine model is team-neutral dark olive, with no map or world marker for either side. Support resupply returns AT mines like any other pouch item. **Bots**: an Engineer bot holding a flag with no target in sight lays a mine in front of itself while an enemy ground hull is within 150 m of the flag (`BotManager.layMine` in `server/bots.js`, at most one check per 15 s, own mines kept 6 m apart). It uses the human throw path.

**Recon gadgets.** With the Recon picked, the loadout slot has the same gadget toggle as the Engineer: **BCN** (SPAWN BEACON) or **MSN** (MOTION SENSOR), riding the deploy intent as `gadget`. Both live in the grenade pouch (types `beacon` and `sensor`, appended to `GRENADE_TYPE_IDS`, `kitOnly`; `KIT_POUCH_GADGETS` in `shared/conquest-kits.js`), so they reuse the pouch, the Ready Card and the throw path. Rules, hit boxes and the wire codec are in `shared/recon-gadgets.js` (`RECON_GADGET_RULES`), the authority in `server/modes/conquest/recon-gadgets.js` (`ReconGadgets`, owned by the policy).

- **SPAWN BEACON** (one per life, no resupply): laid like the AT mine, on the floor under the crosshair within 3 m or 1.2 m ahead, with 2 voxels of air above it for the antenna (else NO GROUND, nothing spent); a ghost shows where it goes. It is refused (RESTRICTED, nothing spent, no ghost) inside an enemy HQ's restricted radius and outside the combat area, the zones whose timer kills infantry, so a squad never deploys into that timer (`beaconRestricted`, the same rule on the client and the authority; Recon bots skip such spots). One beacon per Recon: laying a new one removes the old. It survives the owner's deaths and respawns and goes when the owner respawns on another kit or the sensor gadget, changes team or leaves, when its floor voxel is destroyed, and at the round end. The owner's squad (owner included) can deploy on it (see the deploy options above); a squad mate's spawn pays the owner `beacon_spawn` (15). It has 100 HP: enemy bullets (the hitscan walk stops on its 0.4 × 0.84 m box) and blasts (the blast type's damage, linear falloff over its radius) destroy it, and the destroyer earns `gadget_destroyed` (50); friendly fire never hurts it. Everyone sees the case in the world (team-relative lamp, blinking while ready, dim while cooling down); on the map the own team sees it as the antenna glyph, enemies only within 30 m of it or while their team has spotted it (Y marks it like a player). Owner banners: SPAWN BEACON PLACED / DESTROYED.
- **MOTION SENSOR** (two per life, resupplied like any pouch item): thrown like a grenade (no cook), it settles after 1.6 s and senses for 20 s. Every 250 ms it reveals each enemy within 30 m (and 12 m up or down) whose flat speed is at least 2.6 m/s, so a walk (4.4) or sprint gives a body away and a crouch walk (2.2), a prone crawl or standing still does not; a reveal lasts 1.2 s after the scan that made it. Revealed enemies show to the owner's team as red dots with a halo on the minimap and full map; the own team sees the sensor with a dashed 30 m ring, enemies see the puck in the world only. A teammate's kill on a revealed enemy pays the sensor's owner `spot_assist`, unless a spot mark already paid it. At most two live sensors per Recon (a third removes the oldest); 25 HP against bullets and blasts (`gadget_destroyed`).
- **Bots**: a Recon bot's gadget splits by a stable id hash and stays on the beacon while its beacon stands (`BotCommander.gadgetFor`). An attacking or staging Recon 25–150 m short of its flag with no enemy within 30 m lays the beacon (keeping one that already stands within 150 m of that flag); a Recon 8–60 m from the flag it attacks, stages for or defends throws a sensor that settles near the flag centre (the throw is searched with the real grenade integrator) unless one of its own already watches it (`BotRecon.use`, `server/bot-recon.js`, through the human throw path). Squad bots weigh a beacon as a spawn with a 6 m penalty. Any bot with nobody to fight shoots a visible enemy beacon or sensor within 45 m (`BotRecon.shoot`), switching to a gun first.
- Audio: the beacon chirps and the sensor pings (procedural cues, `sfx.reconGadget`); no new voice or sample banks.

## Squad orders and the comm rose

(2026-10-20, contract v6.) Data and shared helpers in `shared/conquest-comms.js` (tuning in `COMM_RULES`), authority in `server/modes/conquest/orders.js` (`SquadOrders`) and `server/modes/conquest/comms.js` (`CommSystem`), bot answers in `server/bot-comms.js`, client models in `public/js/ui/conquest/comms-state.js`. Design: variant A of `docs/design/conquest/orders/` (references, prompts and real captures there).

**Squad orders.** Every squad carries at most one order: a flag. The stance is never stored: an own flag is defended, any other attacked (`orderStance`), so a captured attack order turns into a defend order by itself. Only the squad leader orders, in two ways:

- **Full map** (M, R3, the touch MAP button): click or tap a flag. Clicking the flag that is already ordered clears the order. With a locked pointer or a pad, a cursor on the map does the pointing (mouse / right stick move it, LMB / RT picks) and the camera holds still; members keep their aim and the map tells them that leaders order from it.
- **Comm rose ORDER**: the flag under the crosshair. A flag counts when the view points within 0.18 rad of its pole or inside its zone seen from afar (`atan(radius / distance)`), up to 900 m; of two flags nearly in line the nearer wins. The rose names it ("ATTACK C") and the server resolves it again with the same `flagInSight`.

An order stands only while its giver leads the squad (a new leader starts without one) and every change emits `squad_order`. A squad a bot leads carries the bot commander's flag for that squad, so human members see where their bots are going. Bot members of a human-led squad follow the human's order: the commander takes that squad out of its own flag split and sends it to the ordered flag with the ordinary attack / defend behaviour (staging, squad rides); the first living bot of the squad radios AFFIRMATIVE 0.9 s after the order.

| `order_capture` (100, "ORDER CAPTURE") | each capturer whose squad is ordered onto the captured flag, on top of `capture` | major objective |
| `order_defend` (50, "ORDER DEFEND") | each defender whose squad is ordered onto the defended flag, on top of `defend` | major objective |

**Team livery.** Each hull wears its team's camouflage (WEST woodland, EAST desert) plus saturated recognition panels in the team colour (`recog` in `public/js/vehicles/voxel-model/material.js`: WEST cobalt, EAST signal orange): roof or bonnet panels for aircraft overhead, flank bands, the tank's turret band and rear plate, aircraft fins and wingtips, and rotor blade tips. **Hijacking.** An enemy hull nobody crews can be boarded like an own one (`VehicleSystem.enter` → `hijack`, shared rule `vehicleHijackable` in `shared/vehicle-seats.js`). The hull changes team at once: clients repaint it, old spots, locks and driver credit lapse, and every client gets a `vehicle_hijacked` event (`{vehicleId, type, by, from, to}`) that shows a banner to the hijacker, their team and the team that lost it. A crewed enemy hull can't be boarded, and titans are never hijacked. The interact prompt reads "HIJACK TANK · F1 DRIVER". A hijacked hull's pad keeps its home team (`padTeam` uses `spawn.team` for HQ pads), so when it is destroyed or abandoned it respawns for its original owners.

**Damage.** Damage depends on the weapon class and the armour class (`shared/vehicle-armor.js`). Small arms and MGs do nothing to the tank (its hitmarker is a white spark) but hurt jeeps. Other heavy hulls scale AT by the side that is hit (front ×0.75, side ×1, rear ×1.5, top ×1.3, bottom ×1.5); the tank uses its own armour model, see *Tank armour* below.

- A hull at 25 % HP or less, or one hit by an AT round of at least 30 % of its HP on the rear or bottom (on the tank: the rear plate, the engine deck, the turret bustle or the belly), is **disabled**. It drives at 40 %, slews at half speed, and burns 2 % of its HP per second until it is destroyed or repaired.
- An undamaged hull regenerates up to 60 % after 8 s.
- **Crew repair** (`ConquestRoles._crewRepair`, numbers in `KIT_ROLE_RULES.crewRepair*`): a living Engineer seated in a friendly hull, in any seat, repairs it at 2.5 % of its max HP per second (an outside repair runs at 8 %) once the hull has gone 5 s without damage. It runs on top of the regeneration below 60 %. One Engineer per hull counts, so a crew of Engineers repairs like one. A disabled (burning) hull is not repaired from inside: the disable is what the attacker earned, so the crew must bail out and an Engineer repairs it from outside, which clears the state above 30 %. Aircraft are repaired only while landed (`grounded`), so the STINGER and AA missiles keep their bite in the air; a helicopter can set down to patch up. Titans keep their own shield and repair rules. The repair is credited to the Engineer (`vehicle_repaired` with `by`, welding sparks on clients) and earns a `repair` award per 10 % restored like an outside repair, but at most one every 8 s. While it runs the hull row carries the `repairing` status bit and the crew's vehicle panel shows a green REPAIRING badge with a glowing HP bar.
- Exposed crew can be shot. Crew in a closed hull can't.
- Ground wrecks keep falling until they rest and stay solid for 12 s.
- **Shoving wrecks.** A driven or coasting tank or jeep shoves a solid ground wreck in its way (`VehicleSystem.pushWrecks`, numbers in `VEHICLE_MASS`, `WRECK_PUSH_RULES` and `VEHICLE_RAM`). The impact is perfectly inelastic along the contact normal (jeep 2.5 t, tank 45 t), so the pusher slows by the mass it picks up, and neither hull ever ends faster than the pusher alone (wrecks at most 14 m/s). While the driver holds throttle, the loaded push force (tank 600, jeep 0 t·m/s²) keeps shoving against the wreck's ground drag (1.2 g). A tank carries a jeep wreck at full speed and shoves a tank wreck at under 1 m/s² of acceleration. A jeep only nudges a jeep wreck on impact (8 m/s moves it about 2 m) and is stopped by a tank wreck as by a wall (`maxPushMass` 3 t). A released wreck slides on with friction (`slideWreck`), slides along walls, stops at them, falls off edges, pushes infantry aside (a body pinned against a wall stops it) and sleeps again once at rest. Glancing contacts (under 0.2 alignment), live hulls and aircraft still stop the pusher as before. Aircraft wrecks are not solid, so there is nothing to shove. Clients smooth the moved wreck like any hull row.
- **Hull-on-hull crashes.** A ground hull blocked by another hull (live or wreck) is not hitting a wall: the crash is perfectly inelastic along the contact normal, so each hull feels its mass share of the closing speed, Δv = v_close · m_other / (m_self + m_other) (`VehicleSystem.hullCrash`, `HULL_CRASH_RULES`). Each live hull loses (Δv − 4) / 40 of its max hp, a pair of one team a quarter of that, and a pair crashes at most once per 0.75 s. A tank rear-ending a jeep at 13 m/s feels 0.7 m/s and keeps its hull; the jeep feels 12.3 m/s and loses 67 hp (17 friendly). Two jeeps head-on at 24 m/s each lose 160 hp. Damage to an enemy hull credits the rammer; the rammer's own damage credits the other driver only if that hull drove into it.
- **Ramming blocks.** A moving ground hull whose path is blocked by voxels breaks them when *every* voxel in its way breaks for it (`ramVoxels`). Otherwise it stops as before. Voxels in the bottom layer that don't break are steps and are ignored. A glancing hit (less than 30° into the wall, `RAM_RULES.glancingNormal`) scrapes along the wall as before and breaks only brush, so a tank brushing past a house doesn't carve it. What breaks is `RAM_CLASS` (`shared/world/blocks.js`) against the hull's minimum speed per class (`VEHICLE_RAM.classes`):

  | Class | Blocks | Jeep | Tank |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | brush | leaves, hedges, pine leaves, glass | 2 m/s | 1 m/s |
  | wood | planks, timber fences, crates, wooden props | 8 m/s | 2 m/s |
  | thin | painted siding, accent panels, corrugated steel | never | 3 m/s |
  | masonry | white plaster, brick, soot brick, roof tiles, cobble walls | never | 7 m/s |

  Anything else never breaks: terrain, stone, concrete, metal, sandbags, bedrock and bridges, as well as every block inside an HQ base radius or within 5 m of a flag. Each broken block costs `0.15 · hardness / mass` m/s (a jeep loses 1.1 m/s per plank and 0.1 m/s per leaf block, a tank 0.2 m/s per plaster block) and `(hardness − armor) · 0.5` collision HP (jeep armour 12, tank 55, so a fence post costs a jeep 10 HP and masonry costs a tank 2.5–7.5 HP per block). Where bullet hardness misleads, `RAM_HARDNESS` sets what a ram feels instead: corrugated steel 30 (it stops rounds but folds under a tank, no damage), cobble walls 60 (they topple like plaster). A hull breaks at most 8 (tank) or 4 (jeep) blocks per server tick, drawn from a budget of 40 (12) refilled at 16 (6) per second. When the tick's cap is spent, the hull waits in place with its speed. When the budget is spent, the wall stops it. Broken blocks go through the projectile context's `destroyBlock`, the same path as a rocket crater: `tickBlocks`, `blockRevision` (bot navigation relabels and parked hulls wake), and the client `block` event with debris and the material's break sound. No new audio is involved. Bots don't aim at buildings: their routes avoid them. A bot whose route runs into a hedge or fence simply drives through it. Infantry runover is unchanged.
- **Wall crashes.** A ground hull stopped head-on by a wall it can't break takes collision damage above `max(8 m/s, 0.55 × top speed)`, rising linearly to its top speed. A jeep loses up to its whole hull. A tank (`WALL_CRASH_RULES.tank`) loses at most 10 % (100 HP) and rebounds at 30 % of its impact speed, so at worst it rocks back off concrete or a metal pillar instead of exploding. A glancing hit along a wall is capped the same way.
- Hulls stop every flying projectile, not just rockets. Grenades and unarmed SKIPJACK rounds bounce off them, an armed SKIPJACK round detonates on them as a direct hit, and bolts stop at the face.
- Aircraft collisions only count the speed into the obstacle: a graze slides along the wall, a head-on hit destroys the airframe. Taxiing jets ignore bumps below 15 m/s.

**Tank armour** (`shared/tank-armor.js`, server path `server/sim/vehicle-damage.js`). The tank's collider stays one box, but every hit on it is traced through five detail boxes in the hull frame: the two running gears (up to 0.9 m), the lower tub, the upper hull (skirts, sponsons, glacis and deck, up to 1.6 m) and the turret, which turns with the gun about its pivot. The first face the round enters is the **part** it hits, with that face's outward normal: the upper glacis is sloped 59° back, the lower plate 34°, and the turret's arrow-head cheeks sweep back 45° either side of the mantlet. The hull's pitch and roll tilt every plate (nosing down a slope turns the glacis into a level round; cresting nose-up lays it back). A blast without a direction takes the nearest face square-on.

- **AT rounds** (AP shell, RX-8 HAVOC) penetrate fully while the cosine of the angle to the plate normal is at least the part's plate ratio, and lose damage as `cos / ratio` below it. Below 45 % they **do not penetrate**: 10 % of the part damage, not effective (white spark). At or past the **ricochet angle** (AP 70°, HAVOC 75°, other AT 72°) the round **glances off**: no damage, it leaves the plate mirrored about the normal with 55 % (AP) or 45 % (rocket) of its speed, flies on (`projectileUpdate` re-publishes it) and may detonate elsewhere. A round ricochets at most twice. Only a plate within 0.8 m of the collider face counts for a ricochet; a round that crosses open air beside the turret or over the deck meets the plate behind it square-on.
- **HE** never ricochets. It deals the heavy cell (×0.35) times the part's HE factor times `0.6 + 0.4 · cos`, so sloped front plate shrugs it off and the rear and roofs take more. Splash takes the nearest face square-on. MG and small arms stay at 0.
- **Subsystems.** Dealt damage on a subsystem's parts fills a pool that drains over time (tracks 10, engine 10, turret 15 HP/s). At the threshold the subsystem breaks and the pool empties: **tracks** at 200 (a HAVOC or AP hit on the running gear) stop the hull for 6 s, no drive and no pivot; the **engine** at 250 (one AT hit on the rear plate or engine deck) caps the drive at 50 % for 15 s; the **turret ring** at 340 (an AP hit on the turret side or mantlet, a HAVOC on the turret side) slows the traverse and elevation of the main gun to 40 % for 8 s. An Engineer fixes a broken subsystem after 100 repaired HP (about 1.25 s of repair), and a destroyed tank respawns whole. The row's `st` carries `tracksHit` 512, `engineHit` 1024 and `turretHit` 2048, shown as the badges TRACKS BROKEN, ENGINE DAMAGED and TURRET DAMAGED.
- **Feedback.** `vehicle_hit` on a tank adds `part`, `res` (`rico` or `nopen`, omitted for a penetration) and `sys` (the subsystem the merge window broke). The shooter reads a callout under the crosshair: RICOCHET, NO PENETRATION, TRACKS HIT / ENGINE HIT / TURRET HIT, or the struck part (HULL REAR, TURRET SIDE) for a penetrating AT or HE round; ricochets and non-penetrations get the white spark hitmarker. The struck crew reads RICOCHET · ARMOUR HELD, TRACKS BROKEN, ENGINE DAMAGED or TURRET DAMAGED. A ricochet throws a hot spark fan and plays the ricochet whine over a light plate ping (`sfx.vehicleRicochet`, existing `cq.ricochet` and `cq.hull.small` takes), with a small shake for the crew.
- **Hits to kill** (`node tools/tank-armor-ttk.mjs`: level rounds at random points over the silhouette from ±30° sectors, mean hits to 0 HP, old facing-zone model → this model): AP shell front 2.98 → 3.43, side 2.82 → 2.92, rear 2.03 → 2.29, all-round 2.58 → 2.80; HAVOC front 3.79 → 4.11, side 3.00 → 3.19, rear 2.31 → 2.55, all-round 3.02 → 3.09; HE all-round 11 → 10.8 (front 13.0, rear 9.2). The F keys are fixed (not rebindable) and are swallowed while you are near or inside a hull, so F5 never reloads the page. On touch, SEAT moves to the next free seat; on a gamepad, D-pad up does.

**Seat takeover.** A human who deploys into, enters (T or F*n*) a seat held by a friendly bot takes it (`VehicleSystem.botTakeover`, the same rule on the server and in the shared `deployOptions`). The bot leaves through the normal exit: beside the hull on the ground, or bailing out of an airborne aircraft, where it opens its parachute itself (a jet's bot pilot leaves on the ejection seat). A bot never takes a human's seat and a human never puts out another human. Taking the pilot seat of an airborne aircraft hands over without a lurch: until the new pilot's client sends its first mounted control packet (at most `SEAT_TAKEOVER_GRACE_SECONDS` = 1 s), the server flies neutral stick, keeping attitude, altitude hold and throttle, so the hull neither drops nor turns toward a stale camera.

### Bots riding with humans

A hull that a living human drives or pilots takes nearby bots aboard (`BotCommander.planHitches`, part of the 2 Hz plan; the rules live in the `HITCH_*` constants in `server/bot-commander.js`).

- **Seats**: free seats only, gunner seats first (jeep HMG, tank commander RWS, helicopter chin gun, transport door guns), then passenger seats. A bot never takes a seat a human holds, and never the driver or pilot seat. A seat already given to a crew or a squad ride is skipped.
- **Who**: first the driver's squad mates within 60 m, as many as there are seats. Squad mates booked on a squad ride that has not left yet switch to the human's ride. Then the nearest other teammates within 45 m, with at most ceil(team bots / 4) of them riding with humans per team. A bot stays where it is while it stands in a flag it is taking or one that is being fought over, holds a threatened flag (it stands in it, or its squad defends it and it is within 60 m), is on an all-in home defence, is crewing a hull, or is running a revive or repair.
- **When**: bots only set out for a ground hull moving slower than 3 m/s, or an aircraft on the ground. A hull standing at a flag the bots want takes nobody new. A booked bot that has not boarded within 20 s, or falls more than 80 m behind, gives up.
- **Riding**: riders man their mounts while the human drives (passengers sit). In the air they stay aboard until landing; if the human bails out, the pilotless rule applies (they bail out after 1.5 s).
- **Getting out**: once the hull stops (below 0.8 m/s; aircraft landed) within the radius + 12 m of a flag they want (not owned by the team, threatened, or their squad's order flag), the riders get out. They also get out when the human leaves the driver seat, or when the hull has stood still for 30 s away from such a flag. A dropped rider walks for 30 s (60 s after the idle drop) before it hitches again.

### Bots waiting for humans

A bot that drives or pilots a hull on an order (a crew, or a squad ride's driver) waits for a human teammate who runs to it, like a Battlefield squad mate (`BotCommander.planWaits` in the 2 Hz plan; the drivers read `holdFor` every tick; the rules live in the `WAIT_*` constants in `server/bot-commander.js`).

- **Who is waited for**: a living human of the team on foot within 50 m (and 12 m in height) who closes in at 1.2 m/s or more, moving toward the hull (at least 60 % of the speed points at it) or looking at it (within 0.6 rad). The hull needs a seat the human may take besides the driver's: free, or held by a bot (seat takeover). The jet has no such seat and never waits. One wait per hull and per human; the driver's squad mates come first, then the nearest human.
- **Which hulls wait**: a ground hull standing still, or one that has gone less than 10 m since it last stood still (it brakes to a stop); a hull farther along never stops or turns back. An aircraft only on the ground: a held transport or helicopter keeps its rotors idle on the ground (no lift) until the wait ends. An airborne aircraft never waits.
- **While waiting**: the driver brakes and stands; every gun keeps working. A squad ride's departure, a tank's overwatch move and a takeoff are put off.
- **End**: the human boards (the hull leaves at once: `holdFor` lets go on the next tick, without waiting for the plan), dies or goes down, gets into another hull, walks away (1 m/s or faster away from the hull, outside 8 m), drifts beyond 60 m, or stops closing in for 1.5 s while outside 8 m (standing within 8 m counts as boarding). Otherwise the wait ends after 8 s, or 12 s for the driver's squad mate. A human given up on (timed out, turned away, took another hull) is not waited for again for 30 s, so nobody can park a bot by standing next to it.
- A squad ride no longer waits for a booked rider whose seat someone else (the human) took, so the ride leaves as soon as the human is aboard. If the human takes the driver seat instead, seat takeover applies and the bots aboard ride with the human (above).

### Bot marksmanship

Conquest bots miss more, and less evenly, than in the other modes (playtest 2026-10-14: on Frontier's long sightlines a bot that had fought for a few seconds landed nearly every round). On top of the shared aim model (`server/bot-aim.js`: eased steering, correlated wander, simulated recoil) Conquest infantry (`CQ_AIM` in `server/bots.js`; not Titanfall) gets:

- **An error floor**: the aim error still shrinks as a fight wears on, but only to 0.8 of its opening size (0.55 elsewhere).
- **Burst bias**: one offset (1.0 × the current aim error, unit `gaussish` spread) is drawn when a contact is recognised and after every burst, and held through the next burst, so whole bursts can sail past while the next one connects.
- **Lead slip**: each burst misjudges a moving target's lead by a held 0.3 s × `gaussish` of its motion, at full size from 40 m and scaled down linearly inside it.
- **Flinch**: every hit taken jolts the view by up to 2.2° in a random direction, recovered by skill like recoil.

Crew gunners and bot pilots (`CQ_MOUNT_AIM` in `server/bot-vehicle-combat.js`, used by `server/bot-aircraft.js`) add 0.35° to their skill-scaled error and hold a burst bias (1.2 × that error) re-rolled every 0.9–1.8 s; the trigger waits for the mount (or the airframe) to sit on the biased point, so the bias lands as a miss. The STINGER and the jet's AA missile keep their seeker on the hull centre. Measured with a pinned rifle bot against an idle enemy on Frontier (8 seeds; first 2.5 s after the first shot → after 7 s): Normal at 40 m 0.50 → 0.32 and 0.87 → 0.66 hits per shot, at 70 m 0.14 → 0.02 and 0.51 → 0.28, at 15 m 0.79 → 0.84 and 0.93 → 0.82. In the action gate (seeds 1–6) the infantry hit rate fell from about 0.37 to 0.24 per shot.

**Bots and guided missiles.** The commander crews the AT jeep like a tank (`planCrews` in `server/bot-commander.js`: driver priority 3, gunner priority 5 alongside the tank gunners); its bot driver uses the tank's overwatch driving (`OVERWATCH` in `server/bot-vehicle-driving.js`: it stands 40–80 m from the focus flag with line of sight, faces the threat, moves every 6–10 s and backs out when hurt) instead of the jeep's squad runs, and like the jeep it reverses rather than turns when its next point lies behind it. A bot gunner (`server/bot-vehicle-combat.js`) scores a beam weapon by target (`BEAM_SUITABILITY`: ground hulls 1.2, aircraft 0.4, infantry 0.35, so the chin cannon keeps infantry and the missile takes armour) and only within `beamMissileRange` (85 % of the missile's flight, about 510 m for GUIDED MSL and 500 m for TOW); an empty launcher scores ×0.25. It aims through the sight mount (the chin gimbal for the helicopter) straight at the hull with no lead (the missile flies to the sight, so leading it by its flight time, as the bots did before 2026-10-20, steered it 12–38 m past a crossing hull), and after a missile leaves it holds the crosshair on that target for the flight time (`guideUntil`, at most 9 s): no sweeps, no weapon switch and no new target until the missile lands. While its own missile is still riding the beam short of the target's range plus 15 m (`beamMissileInFlight`; one that flew past has missed and frees the gunner) the gunner keeps the missile selected and aims for it, and a chin cannon that is still selected holds fire, so a cannon lead never drags the sight off the target. The AT jeep counts as a light hull with threat 1.7 in target choice.

### Bot capture flow

The commander re-plans every 500 ms, and on Frontier (flags 176-275 m apart) a re-plan that flipped a squad between two flags mid-walk (the stalemate flank toggling with the contested line, or a lane swap as squad centroids moved) left it wandering between them without reaching either; a bot inside a zone left it the moment its squad's order changed. Three rules in `server/bot-flag-flow.js` (`FLAG_FLOW_RULES`, called from `BotCommander.planTeam` and `computeGoal`) keep bots on their captures. Every input is authoritative mode, entity and team-knowledge state.

- **Order commitment** (`keepAttackOrder`): an attack order keeps its flag for 45 s against a re-plan to another attack (a flank order 75 s). Squad bots standing in the zone extend that up to twice as long and also hold it against a defend call or a new flank. The squad is freed at once when the flag is taken, by a human squad leader's order (squad orders), by an all-in home defence, by a defend call or a stalled line's flank while nobody of the squad stands in the zone, and when the commitment runs out.
- **Staying on a capture** (`committedFlag`): a bot on foot in the zone (0.85 r, 8 m height band) of a flag its team does not own stays there, whatever its squad's order says, while its team counts at least one body there and at least as many as the enemy; the same holds for an owned flag being neutralized or fought over. A defend order elsewhere only pulls it out while more than 0.4 of the control swing to the next transition is left (`turnRemaining`; a half swing alone takes 8 s). The stay drops the recon overwatch and the staging hold for that bot.
- **Passing flags** (`passingFlag`): a bot on foot on an attack or a plain walk (never on a defend order or an order from a human squad leader) that passes within 40 m of the edge of an enemy or neutral flag no enemy is known to hold takes it on the way; once two of its team stand in that zone, further passers keep to their order.
- **Back-caps** (`BotCommander.planTeam`): a line flag fought over for 10 s (`STALEMATE_FLANK_MS`), or an attack with no ownership change for 25 s (`ATTACK_STALL_MS`), sends one free squad (with two or more free) to the least-defended enemy flag behind the line. It was 20 s and 40 s; the earlier flank is most of the gain below.
- **Supports trail the leaders** (`computeGoal`): a Support on an attack keeps 5 m behind the squad mates that lead the push, never behind a centroid that holds itself, another Support, a Medic, a Recon on overwatch or a mate walking to a hull. A squad of trailers used to step back from its flag 5 m at a time (each trailing a centroid that held the others) and walked away from the order; with no leader on foot the Support walks to the flag.
- **Valid squad spawns** (`deployFor`): a dead bot takes a squad spawn only when the deploy system would accept it (`DeploySystem.validate`). A mate hit in the last 4 s (`squadSpawnDamageLockMs`) or on its spawn cooldown used to win the pick, be refused at respawn and put the bot at the HQ, 300-440 m from the fight, instead of on the nearest valid flag.

**Medic bots** (`server/bot-medic.js`, `MEDIC_BOT_RULES`). With no body to revive and no hurt mate, a Medic trails one mate so the heal aura (6 m) covers it while it walks, holds cover or stands in the zone (at a staging point it keeps its own slot of the staging ring): the nearest living squad mate on foot within 140 m, else a teammate of another squad bound for a flag within 30 m of its own (+25 m of score), never another Medic, a Recon only as a last choice (+45 m). It does not walk back to a mate more than 30 m farther from the flag than itself (a fresh HQ spawn) unless that mate is within 15 m. The spot is 2.5 m behind a standing mate on the side away from the flag (reached within 1.5 m) and leads a moving mate by 1.2 s of its ground velocity, so the Medic sprints on its heels: the goal carries `escort`, and an escort point never counts as near the objective, which would hold the bot to a walk. A Medic standing in a zone it is taking, or in a defend surge, only trails mates inside the zone. Medics take a squad spawn whenever it is at least as close to the objective as the best flag spawn (others need it 45 m closer), and the heal walk to a hurt mate stops on its heels (`medicTrailSpot`) instead of on top of it.

**Pit escape** (`server/bot-unstick.js`, `UNSTICK_RULES`). ### Falling, parachutes and the ejection seat

All values live in `shared/parachute.js`; the server (`server/sim/movement.js`) and the client prediction (`public/js/player-physics.js`) run the same state machine.

- **Fall damage** (`FALL_DAMAGE`): landing damage from the downward impact speed `v` (gravity 24 m/s², a fall of h m lands at √(48·h)). Up to 16.5 m/s (≈5.7 m: jumps, stairs, 3–4 m terrace drops even when jumped off) is free; above it damage is `100·(v − 16.5)/(26.5 − 16.5)`: 10 m ≈ 54, 15 m and more is lethal. Water at least 2 blocks deep under the landing point cushions it completely; an open canopy lands at 5 m/s and never hurts. A lethal fall within 5 s of damage from an enemy credits that enemy (kill feed "ENEMY ⤓ FELL VICTIM"), otherwise it is a self death (killer `''`, "⤓ FELL VICTIM", killer card "YOU DIED · FELL"). Both use the kill key `fall` (`CONQUEST_DEATH_KEYS`); the body stays revivable.
- **Parachute** (`PARACHUTE`): press Jump while falling with at least 6 m of air below (Space, the pad's jump, or the touch JUMP button, which reads CHUTE when it would open one). The canopy sinks at 5 m/s, glides 3 m/s along the look direction and WASD adds up to 4 m/s, so look and keys steer it. Jump again cuts it (it can be reopened while still 6 m up); landing or water closes it. A ledge grab wins over opening it, and the coyote window after walking off an edge never opens it. No weapon fires and no sights open under the canopy (`ConquestPolicy.canFire`); the HUD prompt shows "OPEN PARACHUTE" / "CUT PARACHUTE" with the Jump key. Leaving any aircraft in flight, or any long fall, works this way.
- **Ejection seat** (`EJECTION`, jet only): the pilot leaving an airborne jet (T) is fired out along the airframe's up axis blended with world up at 26 m/s, keeps 60 % of the jet's velocity (≤ 30 m/s horizontally), rides the seat ballistic for 1 s (no air control, a 0.6 s rocket plume) and then the canopy opens automatically. The canopy glass flies off and a pilot's first-person camera gets a short shake. The empty jet flies on and crashes like any crewless aircraft. On the ground (landed and below 4 m/s) the jet is an ordinary exit.
- **Crashes**: crew of a hull wrecked by its own collision or fall (no enemy damage behind it) die with the kill key `crash` and no killer ("⤓ CRASHED VICTIM"), instead of a "ROADKILL" credited to their own driver.
- **Bots** open their canopy themselves once a fall would hurt (falling faster than 10 m/s with at least 2.5 m below and a predicted impact above the safe speed), never cut it, and land unhurt. Riders bail out of an airborne aircraft that has had no pilot for 1.5 s (`BAIL_PILOTLESS_MS`, e.g. after a human pilot ejected). Bot crews stay aboard a burning aircraft: a long canopy ride out of the fight cost the action gate more infantry kills than the quick redeploy.
- **Presentation**: every body under a canopy shows a voxel canopy with suspension lines (own team blue, enemy orange), the seat ride shows a seat with a smoke and flame plume, and the seat tumbles away when the canopy opens (`public/js/vehicles/parachute-fx.js`, owned by `VehicleFx`). In first person the own canopy rides 2.2 m higher and 1.3 m behind the eye (`PARACHUTE_FX.firstPerson`): a level view shows only the thin risers, looking up shows the canopy. The viewmodel stows the gun muzzle-down out of the frame while under the canopy or on the seat (`stowed` in the rig context).

Mouse aim is a War Thunder-style instructor. The mouse moves a free world aim direction (`VehicleController.flightAim`, ±1.3 rad pitch; Q/E turn it at 0.9 rad/s); MOUSE FLIGHT SENSITIVITY and INVERT MOUSE FLIGHT PITCH apply to it. The chase camera looks along the aim with no hull lag (the hull rides a little below the centre), so the screen centre is the aim circle. The HUD draws the nose marker where the hull and its fixed guns point (the jet's gun cross with its funnel, the helicopter's rocket pip, a small cross for the transport), joined to the circle by a dotted line, and hides the infantry crosshair. Nothing new travels on the wire: the aim is the ordinary input `yaw`/`pitch`. The jet sends no stick axes, so the server's attitude autopilot in `shared/vehicle-handling/plane.js` (`AIM_INSTRUCTOR`) flies it: a bank whose coordinated turn rate closes the heading error (gain 2/s, damped by the turn already under way), a rudder trim inside 0.2 rad, the elevator onto the aim pitch, and a stall guard that keeps the nose near the flight path when the airspeed it is heading for drops toward the stall. Rolling out it counter-banks at most 0.05 rad (levelling the wings already brakes the turn) and damps the roll rate, so the nose stops on the aim without a wobble. The bank is never steeper than the wing can carry at the current speed (the load it lifts at 0.85 of the stall angle, `turnAlpha`), and in a bank the elevator also trims the flight path toward the aim pitch (8 × the error, at most 0.4 rad of nose, scaled by how far the wings are banked), so a held turn neither sinks nor balloons; straight flight keeps the nose (and the guns) on the aim. A/D (`vehicleSteer`) override the bank. The helicopters send pitch/roll axes (W/S as `vehicleThrottle`, A/D as `vehicleRollControl`) but no yaw axis, so the nose follows the aim yaw at the pedal rate. This instructor runs on the server because it closes its loop on the exact hull state; a client loop would steer on a snapshot 100–200 ms old.

The bank used to be limited only by `maxBank`, so a slow held turn asked for more lift than the wing had and sank; now the instructor banks only as far as the wing can lift (`acos(1/load)`), and a held turn never stalls. Three pilot assists (`PILOT_ASSISTS` / `AIM_INSTRUCTOR` in `shared/vehicle-handling/plane.js`):

- **Ground proximity (mouse aim).** The server passes `groundClearance`, the height above the highest terrain under the jet and under its next 1, 2 and 3 s of track (`VehicleSystem.advanceAircraft`), and the instructor lets the flight path sink no faster than (clearance − 10 m) / 2 s (a climb below 10 m). Under that floor it levels the wings and pulls up whatever the aim. A 46° dive at 60 m/s held into the ground starts its pull-out at about 90 m and bottoms out at about 10 m (before: it hit the ground); a low pass at 4 m climbs back to about 10 m. A landing (36 m/s or slower, or the throttle at 30 % or less and 46 m/s or slower; both fade out over the next 7–10 m/s) may still sink at 4 m/s, so an idle approach touches down as before. Stick layouts and bots get no ground assist.
- **Stall guard power (mouse aim).** While the stall guard is working the engine spools up toward full power (the throttle setting stays where the pilot left it), unless the pilot is closing the throttle (S), braking, or within 20 m of the ground. Aiming straight up at idle from 45 m/s the jet now climbs about 190 m in 15 s at about 27 m/s instead of sinking 116 m.
- **Alpha limiter (stick flight: mouse stick, keyboard, pad, touch, bots).** A nose-up stick fades out between 0.18 and 0.27 rad angle of attack (with 0.15 s of pitch-rate lead) and pushes the nose down past that, so a full pull at 30 m/s in a 60° bank peaks at 0.24 rad instead of stalling for 1.2 s at 0.37 rad. A nose-down stick is never limited.

Bots fly their own stick axes (`planeAxes`, bank capped at 1 rad) at 28–36 m/s, and both their boundary box (`jetBox`) and the server's map-edge guidance use the turn at a 1.05 rad bank, which the new limits do not change. `tools/conquest-plane-handling-test.mjs` pins the limits, the settle times, the sustained turn at 60 and 40 m/s (rate, radius, sink, no stall), the nose response, the ground pull-up, the idle landing, the stall-guard power and the alpha limiter.

**Airspace.** Frontier's aircraft are not boxed in by the 768 m map. `shared/world/airspace.js` defines an outer valley (the *apron*) 800 m past every edge: a deterministic heightfield on 8 m cells (16 m terraces past 320 m) that starts at the map rim's own height and rolls into a 45-95 m highland of meadows, fields and pine woods (canopy in the ground height, so a low pass through the woods is a crash), cut by the river that leaves the map north and south. `createStateApi.getBlock` answers every out-of-map query inside that limit from the apron (`mapApron(mapId, dimensions)`, memoised), on the server and in the client singleton (`deserializeWorld(bytes, { mapId })`), so hull sweeps, wreck falls, rays, locks, cameras, AGL and parachutes all meet the same ground; past the limit (and on every map without an apron) the edge is still the solid wall. The apron is not voxel state: `setBlock` rejects it, `destroyBlockDirect` returns false there and `pushBlockDelta` ignores out-of-map cells (their flat index would wrap onto a real voxel). Infantry can walk out onto it but run the combat-area timer as before; ground hulls stay on the map. Aircraft crew outside the combat airspace (the map grown by `combat` = 480 m) run the 10 s out-of-bounds timer (`BoundsSystem`, `airArea`); its expiry destroys the aircraft through `VehicleSystem.destroy(v, null, { crewKey: 'restricted' })`, the crew dying with the `restricted` key, and the HUD countdown reads OUT OF BOUNDS · TURN BACK OR LOSE THE AIRCRAFT. Bot pilots fly inside the combat airspace less `botMargin` (120 m): their `jetBox` and the server's `flightArea` for a bot driver stop there, so bots never run the timer. Projectiles live out to the limit. The client draws the apron as one indexed heightfield (`public/js/engine/airspace-apron.js`, ~45k vertices, the backdrop's baked light and aerial fog), the horizon ranges stand beyond the limit (`buildMapBackdrop(..., { airspace })` with the config's coarser `airspace` cells), the far plane reaches 2.8 km (fade 2150-2740 m) and the big map pins aircraft beyond the map to its edge.

**Map edges.** `VehicleSystem.advanceAircraft` steers aircraft back from the edge of their flight area (`flightArea`: the airspace limit on Frontier, the map elsewhere, the bot area for bot pilots) in two layers. A soft steer blends in only near an edge the aircraft is moving toward: a jet needs its turn radius × (1 − sin a) to turn parallel to an edge it approaches at angle a from the normal (nothing when skimming along it, the whole radius head-on) plus its roll-in travel, and the steer ramps in over the 60 m + 0.5 s of travel before that. It pulls a mouse-aim (or bot attitude) request toward the map centre, blends a centre-turn bank (and, deep in, a level-flight elevator) into a manual stick, and a fast jet heading steeply out airbrakes (`speedBrake`, which leaves the throttle setting alone). Rotors hover-brake once their outward drift needs the room left. The hard safety pilot (which takes the controls) engages only inside 60 % of the jet's need, or for a rotor 8 m from the edge after a second's travel, and levels the wings of a jet scrubbed slow or sinking fast. Which way a jet turns is decided by flying both turns ahead (a second of roll-in, then the bank turn until the nose points at the centre) and taking the larger closest approach to the edges; only head-on, where both are alike, does it keep a turn already begun. The soft steer turns the same way when the centre is behind (the shorter way could lead through the edge, and on a 768 m map a fast jet's full turn circle leaves the map either way). It hands back once the jet flies an inward course with its full turn need clear: at once for a mouse-aim pilot who aims back into the map, after levelling the wings for a stick pilot, otherwise on a course the soft steer would leave alone; the pilot's own throttle is restored. The vehicle row carries `autopilot: true` while it flies and `edgeSteer` (0.5–1) while the soft steer pulls hard, and the pilot HUD shows an amber MAP EDGE · AUTOPILOT / TURN BACK chip above the aim (above the stall chip; above the tapes on phones). A jet that hits the edge itself swings its nose into the remaining along-edge motion (the edge only stops the outward axis), so it regains airflow instead of sliding along nose-first. From the middle of Frontier a full-speed jet keeps its controls on every heading; only head-on toward an edge does the soft steer begin to blend in.

**Resting off the gear.** A jet slower than 4 m/s that lies on a wingtip, its nose or its tail within 2 m of the ground (or stands tilted on its gear, wedged in a map corner) has no airflow over its control surfaces, and used to keep that attitude for good. It now tips back toward level at 1.5 rad/s, easing up and toward the map centre (at most 15 cm up and 1 m in) where the levelling wing would meet a voxel or the edge. The dug-in airframe does not slide while it is tilted and settles at no more than 2 m/s.

On a gamepad, D-pad left works like T: a tap enters or exits and a hold revives or repairs. D-pad right spots on a tap and opens the comm rose when held for 0.3 s (the right stick picks, the release sends), and R3 toggles the full map when you are not scoped. In a vehicle, D-pad up switches to the next free seat, LB fires flares or smoke, Y selects the next weapon and D-pad down cycles the camera view (the grenade pouch stays shut while seated). On the deploy screen, D-pad up and down pick the spawn, LB and RB pick the kit, Y flips the CLASSES / VEHICLES tab and A deploys; on the VEHICLES tab LT and RT move the focus ring between the hull cards and LB and RB step the focused hull through STOCK and its unlocked upgrades (locked ones are skipped). The keyboard mirrors it: the up and down arrows pick the spawn, left and right the class or the focused hull's upgrade, C and V open CLASSES and VEHICLES, Q and E move the hull focus, and Enter deploys.

## Weather

The weather changes during a match, and it changes play. The server draws one seeded timeline per match (`weatherTimeline` in `shared/conquest-weather.js`), steps it with the match clock (`server/modes/conquest/weather.js`) and sends the current mix in every snapshot as `match.conquest.wx`. Every gameplay effect is a multiplier of the state, read from that same quantized tuple on the server and in the browser, so the HUD shows the numbers the server uses.

| State | HUD | Spot range | Lock range | Bot sight | Bot recognition | Canopy drift | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `golden` | CLEAR | × 1 | × 1 | × 1 | × 1 | 0.5 m/s | the authored Frontier evening |
| `overcast` | OVERCAST | × 0.92 | × 0.95 | × 0.92 | × 0.95 | 0.9 m/s | grey deck, flat light |
| `mist` | FOG | × 0.55 | × 0.7 | × 0.62 | × 0.8 | 0.2 m/s | dense valley fog |
| `storm` | STORM | × 0.7 | × 0.75 | × 0.72 | × 0.85 | 1.8 m/s | slate deck, rain, wet ground, lightning |

- **Spot range** scales `spotRange` (300 m) and `spotRangeRecon` (400 m): in a storm an Assault spots out to 210 m, a Recon to 280 m. A hull's THERMAL OPTICS or SEARCH RADAR upgrade (450 m) sees through the weather as it sees through smoke, and the HUD tab shows that range to its crew.
- **Lock range** scales the STINGER (320 m) and the jet's AA missile (350 m) in `server/sim/vehicle-locks.js`; the Titanfall Archer is not affected. Countermeasures, lock time and the cone are unchanged.
- **Bots** see less far and recognise a seen enemy more slowly (`server/bot-weather.js`, read by `observeBotTarget` / `observeBotVehicle` for infantry, crews and pilots). AA Engineers and air crews hold fire beyond the scaled lock range. The mobile AA's radar range is not scaled, but recognition through it still is.
- **Canopy drift**: an open parachute drifts downwind on top of its glide and steering (`stepChuteVelocity`, server and client prediction).
- **Lightning** is presentation only: a strike never damages anything.

**Timeline** (`WEATHER_RULES`, times in match clock). Every forecast script opens clear for 4 to 5.5 minutes (so the first minutes and the 180 s action gate play as before), then holds each state for its `holdMs` (fog 100 to 160 s, storm 150 to 220 s) with 45 to 75 s smoothstep transitions; the last state holds to the end. The four scripts are `golden → overcast → storm → overcast → golden`, `golden → mist → golden → overcast → storm`, `golden → storm → overcast → golden → mist` and `golden → overcast → mist → overcast → storm → golden`. The wind direction is seeded and veers up to 35° over 5 minutes. Ground wetness rises with rain (soaked after 45 s of full rain) and dries over 150 s.

**Lightning**: one draw every 4 to 13 s, kept with the mix's `lightning` value as the chance (so only while a storm is in the mix). A strike lands anywhere within 160 m of the combat area and becomes a `weather_strike {x, y, z}` event (y is the ground under it). The browser flashes the sky, draws one jagged bolt and plays the thunder delayed by distance; the HUD weather tab flashes with it. A stalled server never fires a backlog.

**Mode rule `weather`**: `false` / `'off'` turns weather off (no `wx`, every multiplier 1); a state id (`'storm'`) pins that state for the whole match (tests and captures); `{ seed, script, state }` fixes them; anything else draws a fresh seed per match. Without the rule the server reads `VB_CONQUEST_WEATHER`: `off`, `false`, `0`, `no` or `none` turn weather off, a state id pins it, unset draws the timeline. A match without a finite time limit gets a one-hour timeline (its last state then holds).

**Presentation** (`public/js/engine/conquest-weather-view.js`, tiers in `docs/graphics.md`): the view eases toward the server mix and blends the four Frontier mood presets (`map-atmosphere.js`; on Redrock Canyon the clear state is the map's own desert or dusk mood) over the live world through uniforms only, adds a rain streak field, darkens wet ground (`TERRAIN_WET`), and scales the render-distance fog. A mid-match join snaps to the current weather. The soundscape adds a rain bed, storm gusts and thunder (`docs/audio/conquest-sfx.md`). Design and captures: `docs/design/conquest/weather/`.

## Iron Bridge collapse

Flag C's bowstring truss can be blown down (Battlefield "levolution"). The rules and geometry are data in `shared/conquest-bridge.js` (`IRON_BRIDGE`, `IRON_BRIDGE_RULES`); the server half is `server/modes/conquest/bridge.js`, created by `ConquestPolicy` and inert on any world without the bridge deck.

- **Supports**: the mid-river piers and the four concrete truss feet on the banks share one integrity pool of 1000. Every explosion (`ProjectileSystem.explode` → `ModeController.onBlast`) within max(its damage radius, 1.5 m) of a support takes that weapon's `supportDamage` off the pool, falling off linearly with the distance to the nearest support: RX-8 rocket, guided and TOW missiles 100, tank HE 120, AP 65, jet bomb 420, AT mine 150, helicopter rocket 30, Support mortar HE 40, grenadier MGL 25, frag 15, claymore 10 (its projectile type is `limpet`), mobile AA 35 mm flak 4. Bullets, smoke, molotovs, the chin cannon and the rest do nothing. So 10 rockets, about 8 HE shells, 3 bombs, 25 mortar rounds or 250 flak shells drop it: the mortar and the flak gun can wear the bridge down, but slowly.
- **Stages**: at 50 % the bridge is *damaged*. At 0 it is *failing*: it groans for 3.5 s (`warnMs`) and still carries traffic, then it is *down*. Each stage sends one `bridge` event and is mirrored in `match.conquest.br` for late joiners. Blasts after the last blow change nothing.
- **The fall**: every block of the span over the river (deck, girders and the truss between the banks, up to the arch crown) is doomed through the structural system (`StructureSystem.bringDown`, docs/structural-physics.md): one creak, then up to 16 chunks drop into the river and land as rubble below the surface. The pier stumps under the water turn into river water first. The truss stubs on the banks keep standing, and the few footprint columns that stand on the irregular bank keep a ledge of deck on the quay. Whoever dealt the final blow scores **BRIDGE DESTROYED** (200). Infantry still on the span as it drops take 45 collapse damage, credited to the final blow like a structural collapse: a teammate of that player is spared, as their shots would spare them, and the player's own collapse hurts them. Ground hulls on it (and aircraft parked on the deck, not ones hovering over it) lose 20 % of their maximum HP. Structural collapses the drowned piers set off are credited to the final blow too.
- **After**: the gap is ordinary terrain change. The infantry surface graph learns it from the block deltas (bots swim or walk to a ford). Flag C stays capturable from both banks: the bridgeheads stand inside its radius, and its spawn cells are all off the river. A rematch restores the world and the bridge.
- **Bots**: from the groan on, the road crossing is closed for bot drivers. Routes run over the fords, a hull ordered to C stops at the bridgehead on its own bank, and objective points on the span move to the bridgehead on the bot's bank. A bot standing on the failing span runs for its nearer bank first (`server/bot-bridge.js`).
- **HUD** (design `docs/design/conquest/bridge/`): an amber banner IRON BRIDGE DAMAGED · SUPPORTS AT n %; the amber IRON BRIDGE COLLAPSING · GET OFF THE SPAN banner, held until the fall with a draining countdown bar (raised by the `failing` event or, for a player who joins or reconnects during the groan, by the snapshot's `br` stage; once per collapse, `bridgeStateBanner`); the red IRON BRIDGE DESTROYED · VEHICLES CROSS AT THE FORDS banner. The C chip in the top bar gets an amber (red once down) warning badge. On the minimap, the full map and the deploy map, break marks sit on the road either side of the C diamond (pulsing amber while failing, white once down) and the span shows as hazard stripes, then river. The full map names the flag IRON BRIDGE · DOWN.
- **Audio and effects** (`public/js/fx/bridge-fx.js`): metal groans from the structure voices, grit raining off the deck and a rising camera shake through the warning window, then a rumble with dust and water bursts as the span goes (the chunks themselves are `structure-fx.js`). The objective cues reuse the low-tickets stinger for the groan and the neutralized stinger for the fall. There is no dedicated recording or announcer line yet.

## Breakthrough

- Banners: SECTOR n CAPTURED (+refill TICKETS · PUSH TO SECTOR n+1) or SECTOR n LOST (FALL BACK TO SECTOR n+1), BROKEN THROUGH / LINE BROKEN, OVERTIME / ATTACKERS OUT OF TICKETS. The deploy screen names the FORWARD HQ (FLAGS A B), marks FORWARD flags and refuses with NO REINFORCEMENTS LEFT. The match end reads ATTACKERS BROKE THROUGH or DEFENDERS HELD THE LINE, and the result screen scores sectors (`2 / 3`) instead of tickets.

**Audio.** `sector_captured` plays the captured or lost stinger and the Titanfall momentum calls "We are winning this fight" / "We are losing this fight" (`BREAKTHROUGH_CUES`, loaded with the objective WAVs); `sector_overtime` plays the low-tickets stinger to the attackers and the momentum calls to both sides. The last sector leaves the call to victory or defeat. There are no dedicated sector lines ("Sector one captured", "Fall back") yet: they need new Gemini TTS takes.

**Balance.** Tuned on bot-only engine soaks on Frontier (GameEngine and attachBots as in the bots test, 16 bots, up to 30 minutes, seeds 1 to 6): at 30 tickets and a 20-ticket refill the attackers won 4 of 6 seeds and the defenders 2 (matches of 6 to 14 minutes, two of them through an overtime; the first sector fell after 1 to 4.5 minutes). 50 / 30 let the attackers win all 6 in 4 to 15 minutes, 25 / 20 only 2 of 6. With a 15 s grace most defender bots caught behind the C line died to the timer; at 25 s, with the bots' fall-back goal, a soak loses 0 to 4 defenders to it per match. Humans have not played it yet.

## Maps

The host picks the battlefield in the lobby map list. Only the battlefields are offered in Conquest, and Frontier is the default (`mapForMode('conquest', …)`). Both maps publish the same `mapMeta.conquest` v2 shape: 5 flags (A and B WEST, C neutral, D and E EAST), 2 HQs on the same plateaus, the same HQ fleet with the same pad ids (jeep, tank, AT jeep, mobile AA, attack and transport helicopters and the jet per side, plus the five flag pads), airfields, roads and crossings. Frontier adds its four boats on the river (23 hulls); Redrock Canyon's 12 m canyon has no boat lane (19 hulls). So the rules, deploy, bots, commander, HUD and protocol are unchanged, and nothing on the wire names a map beyond the existing `map` id. The differences are all data:

- the plan: `FRONTIER_PLAN` or `REDROCK_PLAN`, through `conquestPlanFor(mapId)` in `shared/world/battlefield-plans.js`;
- the generator and metadata: `shared/world/frontier-sites/` or `shared/world/redrock-sites/`;
- the load mood: `golden | mist | overcast` on Frontier, `desert | dusk` on Redrock. The dynamic weather (*Weather*) runs on both: on Redrock its clear state is the load mood, and overcast, mist and storm blend Frontier's presets over it;
- the minimap overview image;
- the soundscape sites;
- the map events: the Iron Bridge collapse is Frontier's only (Redrock's crossings are a dam crest, a trestle, a bridge and two fords; `IronBridge` stays inert there).

`shared/world/battlefields.js` lists the battlefield ids. Every large-map code path tests `isBattlefieldMap(id)`, never `id === 'frontier'`.

## Loading Frontier

The same pipeline loads Redrock Canyon: the server builds its template just after it starts listening (`VOXEL_BATTLEFIELD_WARM=0` defers it to first use), and the menu derives its metadata in the worker once a lobby picks it.

Joining a Conquest match takes about 0.7 s locally from the JOIN click to the first live frame (it took 5 s), plus the map transfer over the internet. The pipeline is in [docs/development.md](development.md) (*Joining a large map*, *Map frames and the map cache*):

- **Map cache.** The browser keeps Frontier's pristine voxels (2.0 MB, 0.5 MB deflated) in Cache Storage under the server's template fingerprint and announces it at admission. The server then sends a 20-byte reference plus 5 bytes per changed cell (craters, built blocks) instead of the map. Partial block damage still rides the welcome. A server whose Frontier generator changed sends the new template under a new fingerprint. The cache keeps at most 4 MB of templates (memory and storage, oldest evicted first): Frontier and Redrock Canyon (1.56 MB) fit together, and a new Frontier template pushes the stale one out.
- **Spawn first.** Only the 9 × 9 chunks around the spawn are meshed before play; the rest of the detail range streams in nearest first while the deploy screen or the first seconds of play run (the far terrain covers it meanwhile).
- **Off the main thread.** The light volume is classified and baked in a worker from a copy of the voxels; the Frontier metadata is derived in a worker while the menu is idle.
- **Server.** A join into a damaged map costs O(changed cells) instead of re-encoding 47 M voxels on the tick thread.

Measure with `npm run join:profile` (one muted headless browser; through `.conquest-work/heavy.sh` on a shared machine).

## Server load

Every room, socket, ping and deflate callback shares the server's one event loop, so a long synchronous block in one room delays snapshots and RTT in every other room. The work below removes the big blocks without changing a result: spawns, routes and snapshots are the same bit for bit (seeded matches hash identically before and after on 11 mode/map pairs, including a 2-minute 16-bot Conquest match).

- **Spawn picks** (`server/sim/spawn.js`). A pick scores every pool cell against every living enemy, and each pair used to cast a voxel sight ray: O(N²) rays per wave, about 110 000 when a 32-player TDM room starts. Now:
  - candidates (walkable, dry) are cached per pool until the world's `mutationCount` changes;
  - cells are tried best bound first (the score with no enemy in sight) and a cell stops counting sight lines once it can no longer win, so the chosen cell is the full scan's (ties still go to the earliest cell, the variety jitter draws `Math.random` as often and in the same order);
  - each (cell, enemy) sight line is kept while the enemy's eye stays put and the voxels are unchanged, so the picks of one wave cast each ray once.
- **Ground routes** (`server/bot-navigation.js`, flat-floor maps). `closest()` (the walkable grid node in sight of a point) is memoized per point until the grid or the voxels change. It was 37 % of a TDM tick, because bots ask for the same points again. The route search reuses its scratch arrays. A world still equal to its template copies a cached grid instead of sweeping every cell (the sweep was the 26–32 ms first tick of every TDM Harbor room).
- **Boot warm-up** (`server/boot-warmup.js`, `prepareBattlefield`). Before listening, the server prepares Frontier as before and now also builds its bot cover sets, so the first Conquest room clones them instead of sampling about 1000 points per flag. Right after listening it prepares Redrock Canyon the same way, cover sets included, in one block after a room tick (`VOXEL_BATTLEFIELD_WARM=0` defers it to first use). After listening, it sweeps the flat-floor maps' ground grids in 2 ms slices between room ticks (`VOXEL_NAV_WARM=0` skips it); the battlefields are not in that sweep.
- **Smaller fixes**: `solidBelow` without per-call arrays; the mounted-gun sight line is cast only when the gun would fire; the Docker image runs libuv with 8 threads, so logins and password joins (scrypt) can't hold every snapshot deflate.
- **Seeing it**: the server log names each room tick that takes at least `max(25 ms, 2 × tick interval)` (33.3 ms at the 60 Hz tick), at most once per second per room, with the step's phases of 0.5 ms or more, slowest first: `[voxel-blitz] long tick 41.3 ms (tdm/harbor, 32 bodies, 2 rooms; bots 30.1, snapshot 5.2, vehicles 0.8 ms, 3 so far)` (rooms: those ticking in this process; phases: `TICK_PHASES` in `server/game.js`, the last step's times in `engine.tickPhases`). Connection diagnostics add event-loop utilization, GC pauses, `longTicks`, `skippedTicks` and `mergedSends` (docs/development.md, *Connection diagnostics*).
- The log line `ground grids warmed: N maps ...` counts the maps whose grid the warm-up built or found ready (maps without a flat floor are not counted).

Live smoke:

- `npm run conquest:live` (`tools/conquest-live-smoke.mjs [--seconds 120] [--bots 15] [--out <dir>] [--network] [--software]`) plays a real Frontier match in one muted headless browser against 15 bots. It walks through the lobby, HQ spawn, a walk toward a vehicle and then flag A, the full map, a frag explosion and death, and the deploy screen. It then tries to deploy into a jeep driver seat and drive to flag C. If the jeep is taken (bots often crew it first), it falls back to HQ, as the latest saved run did. It ends on the scoreboard.
  - It fails on any page exception, console error, failed request or server stderr line.
  - It writes screenshots and `live-smoke-report.json` (steps, timeline, FPS, chunk stats) to `docs/design/conquest/redesign/captures/live/`.
  - It always closes the browser and the server.
  - It renders on the hardware GPU (ANGLE/Metal flags). With SwiftShader (`--software`) Frontier draws at about 2 fps, the page cannot drain the 60 Hz snapshot socket, and the server's heartbeat drops the client within a minute.
- `node tools/conquest-mouse-flight-smoke.mjs [--bots 0] [--out <dir>] [--software]` checks MOUSE STICK flight in a real Frontier match (one muted headless browser; it selects the stick and reloads, since mouse aim is the default). The run fails unless the authoritative hull climbs more than 25 m, banks past 0.6 rad, turns more than 1 rad and ends with roll under 0.15 rad, vertical speed under 4 m/s and speed above 50 m/s.
